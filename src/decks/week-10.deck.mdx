# Exit, voice and the fork

SLOP6531 · Week 10

---

## June 2023

Reddit announced API pricing that would end third-party clients.

Several thousand subreddits went private, including most of the largest.

The people who closed them were unpaid.

## What that settled

An old question, badly:

- the volunteers had built the communities
- the company held the keys
- nobody had ever written down which of those mattered

---

## Hirschman, 1970

Three responses to an organisation you are unhappy with.

| Response | In practice |
| --- | --- |
| **Exit** | you leave |
| **Voice** | you complain, and stay |
| **Loyalty** | what makes voice worth trying |

---

## Unpaid changes the arithmetic

Exit costs an employee their income.

It costs a volunteer nothing — which is why it is weak.

The leverage is not the leaving. It is the work that stops.

---

{/* _class: quote */}

> A strike withholds labour from an employer.
>
> This withholds it from the queue.

---

## The third option

Leave, and take the work with you.

| Fork | What it needed |
| --- | --- |
| Enciclopedia Libre, 2002 | the text, and it had it |
| MariaDB, 2009 | the code, and the maintainers |
| LibreOffice, 2010 | both, plus a foundation |

---

## Enciclopedia Libre won and lost

Spanish Wikipedia's contributors forked in 2002, over a proposal to run
advertising.

The proposal was dropped. Wikipedia has never carried ads.

The fork outlived the argument by twenty years, and went offline in 2024.

---

## What a fork actually needs

- **the data** — licensed so you can take it
- **the tooling** — or the will to rebuild it
- **the people** — the hard one

Two out of three is a mirror, not a community.

---

## Who bears the cost

You are bargaining with a platform.

For a fortnight, the queue is unattended.

The people who lose are the ones you closed the community to protect.
