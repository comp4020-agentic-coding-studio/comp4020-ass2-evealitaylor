# Policies and support

This course sends students into communities of real people who did not sign up
to be coursework. Most of what follows exists because of that.

## The contributor undertaking

You sign this in week 1 and it binds for the semester. It is four clauses.

1. **You contribute in good faith, for the project's benefit, not for the
   mark.** An edit made to have something to log is vandalism with a
   bibliography. If a week goes by with nothing your project genuinely needs,
   you log the week as empty and say so.
2. **You disclose that you are a student when asked, and volunteer it where the
   community expects it.** Some projects require a declaration on your user
   page; some do not care. The norm map you build in week 2 tells you which
   yours is, and getting it wrong is the first thing that will be held against
   you.
3. **You do not experiment on people.** No deliberate rule-breaking to observe
   the response, no test vandalism, no baiting a moderator to generate material
   for an assessment. The course sandbox exists for anything you want to try
   that a real community would have to absorb.
4. **You clean up after yourself.** If your contribution is reverted, you do not
   leave the talk-page thread unanswered because the marking week has ended.

Breach of clause 3 is academic misconduct and is treated as such. Breaches of
the others are usually a conversation.

## Writing about living people

The Conflict Autopsy asks you to reconstruct a real dispute. The people in it
are alive, searchable, and in most cases identifiable by username even if you
do not give the name.

Write about conduct in the record, not about character. "Their third revert
came ninety minutes after the talk-page reply" is a finding; "they were clearly
acting in bad faith" is a claim about a person's interior you cannot source and
will not be marked kindly for. Use the name the project uses. Do not
cross-reference a username to a real identity that the person has not linked
themselves, and do not contact anyone in your autopsy for comment — you are a
student with an assignment, not a journalist with a duty, and the distinction
matters to the person receiving the message.

If a subject asks you to take something down, tell Prue before you reply.

## Machine assistance

You may use whatever tools you like to draft your own submissions, and you will
be asked to say which and where. This is not a concession; it is consistent with
the position the course argues in week 11.

What you may not do is put generated text into somebody else's project without
checking every claim in it against the project's own standard of evidence. That
is not a course rule. Wikipedia, OSM and most repositories have arrived at
versions of it themselves, for the reason week 11 sets out: producing costs
seconds and assessing costs a volunteer twenty minutes, and the bill lands on
somebody who is not you.

Disclosed assistance is fine. Undisclosed generated content in a real
community's queue is a breach of clause 1.

## Extensions, late work and missed shifts

Written work: extensions are arranged before the deadline, by email to Prue, and
are routinely granted. Late work without an extension loses 5% of the available
mark per day.

Shifts are different, and the difference is the course's whole argument. A
missed written deadline costs you marks. A missed shift costs somebody else
their evening, because the queue does not clear itself and the next person on
the rota inherits it. This is why the week 9 rota names a cover for every slot
before anyone needs one, and why using that cover — in advance, in writing — is
evidence of competence rather than of unreliability.

## Continuity of Service

The School of Invented Disciplines requires every student holding privileged
access on a School system to file a handover note before relinquishing it, under
the Continuity of Service policy (SlopU Pol. 4.11). In this course that means
the week 12 handover, and it is a requirement rather than an assessment item:
the sandbox passes to the following year's cohort, and an undocumented rule is a
rule the next cohort will enforce without knowing why.

The policy was drafted in 2024 by a member of staff who has since left the
School without filing one.

## If the work affects you

Moderation queues contain the things that were removed from communities, which
is to say the worst of what was submitted to them. The course sandbox is small
and the cohort is writing its own rules, so the exposure is far below what a
real queue involves — but week 8 onwards is not neutral material, and the
research on people who do this work professionally is unambiguous about what
sustained exposure does.

You can rotate off a queue at any time, without explanation, by telling your
Shift tutor. It is not marked against you; it is the rota working.

The University counselling service is available to all enrolled students, and
Prue and Ren will both tell you, at some length, that they wish something like
it had been available to their own mod teams.
