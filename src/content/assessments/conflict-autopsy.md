## The brief

> Take one dispute that has already ended, and account for it so well that
> nobody in it comes out as the idiot.

Pick a conflict in a volunteer community that is fully documented in public: an
edit war, a deletion fight, a moderator removal, a fork, a contributor's
departure. It must have a record — talk pages, modlogs, issue threads, arbitration
findings — and it must be over.

Then reconstruct it. Who did what, in what order, and — this is the work — what
each party could see at the moment they acted. Most online disputes are not
disagreements about values. They are two people with different partial views of
a history, each of whom has correctly concluded that the other is behaving
badly.

The third criterion is the one students find hardest. Having explained the
parties, explain the structure: the interface that did not show one of them
something, the rule that was ambiguous, the norm that was never written down,
the queue that was six hours behind. A dispute that could only have been caused
by a bad person is usually a dispute you have not finished reading.

## What you submit

2,000 words, plus a timeline with every claim linked to its source in the
record. Do not pick a dispute you were personally involved in — you will be
doing that in week 4, and it does not work.

Living subjects are real people. The [policies](/policies/) page governs how you
write about them, and it is not optional here.
