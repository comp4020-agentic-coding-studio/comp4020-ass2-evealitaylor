## The brief

> Contribute to a real project every week for ten weeks, and keep a log that a
> stranger could use to learn what you learned.

The log is the spine of this course. Every Shift from week 2 produces something
that goes into it, and by week 11 you have a record of your own apprenticeship:
the norm map, the first edit, the revert and what you did about it, the sourcing
dossier, the closing rationale, the queue shifts, the audit.

The marking weight is deliberately on the annotation rather than on the
contributions. A student whose edits were all accepted and who cannot say why is
in worse shape than one who was reverted six times and understood it by the
fourth. What earns the marks is the gap between what you predicted and what
happened, closing over ten weeks.

Your conduct is part of it, because it is public. We read the record, including
the talk pages. Nobody has ever lost marks here for being wrong; a student lost
marks in 2026 for being right rudely, to a volunteer, in a thread that is still
the first result for their username.

## What you submit

A single document, one entry per week from week 2 to week 11, each carrying:

- a link to the contribution in the project's public history, so the diff
  resolves
- what you expected would happen, written before it did
- what actually happened, including silence
- one paragraph on what you had misunderstood about the community

Ten entries. A missed week is logged as a missed week, with a sentence saying
so, and costs less than a fabricated one.
