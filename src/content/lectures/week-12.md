Most volunteers do not resign. They get busy, then they get tired, then they
stop opening the tab, and eighteen months later somebody notices that the
AutoModerator config has a rule nobody can explain and the only person who knew
why it was added has not logged in since March.

Institutional memory in these projects lives in individual heads and in
archives that are technically public and practically unsearchable. When a
long-serving contributor leaves quietly, the project does not lose their labour
— someone else will do the removals. It loses the reasons.

So the final piece of work is a handover. Write down what you did, what you
decided, what you decided against and why, which rules exist because of a
specific incident, who to ask, and what you would do next if you were staying.
It is the least satisfying document you will produce all semester and the only
one that outlives you.

The School of Invented Disciplines requires it under the Continuity of Service
policy, which was drafted, in the spirit of the thing, by somebody who has
since left the School and cannot be reached for comment.
