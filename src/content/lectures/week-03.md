Wikipedia's advice to a new editor fits on a page and has barely changed since
somebody wrote it down on 17 January 2001, two days after the site opened: be
bold. Make the change. Do not ask permission, do not propose it on the talk
page first, do not wait for consensus that no one is going to assemble on your
behalf.

This is generous advice and it is also a trap, because the sentence that
follows it in practice is: and then be reverted. The cycle has a shortcut,
WP:BRD, and learning to read the second letter as normal rather than as
rejection is most of what separates the ten percent who stay from the ninety
percent who do not.

Underneath the etiquette is a standard of evidence that is stranger than it
looks. Wikipedia does not ask whether your claim is true. It asks whether it
has already been published somewhere a reasonable person would trust, which
means a true statement you know first-hand is inadmissible and a false one in a
newspaper is fine until a better source contradicts it.

You will hate this rule in week 3 and defend it in week 6.
