The interface gives you a button and, if you are lucky, a dropdown of canned
reasons. It does not give you a text box and it does not ask you to justify
yourself, so most moderators never learn to, and the result is an internet full
of people who have been removed from somewhere and have no idea why.

A removal is a message to a person. Write it as one. Name the rule, quote the
part of their post that hit it, say whether the door is open, and stop. Do not
be sarcastic, because it will be screenshotted. Do not explain at length,
because length reads as defensiveness. Do not apologise for enforcing a rule
you believe in.

Then the appeal comes back, and here is where the course convenor takes a turn
on the other side of the desk. In 2014 the Arbitration Committee topic-banned
me from an area I had worked in for eight years. I appealed. The appeal was
declined, correctly, by people applying a process I had spent three years
clerking for. I will walk you through the case, including the parts where I was
obviously wrong and did not think so at the time.

You cannot run a fair queue until you have been in one.
