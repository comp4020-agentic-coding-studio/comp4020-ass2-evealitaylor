English Wikipedia carries around seven million articles. The number of people
who make more than a hundred edits in a given month is roughly thirty thousand.
Divide one by the other and you have the course in a single ratio: a reference
work consulted a billion times a month, maintained by a workforce that would
fit in a football stadium and leave most of the seats empty.

Run the same arithmetic on Reddit, on OpenStreetMap, on the package registry
your last project pulled four hundred dependencies from. The shape holds. A
very small number of unpaid people are doing a very large amount of
unglamorous, repetitive, contested work, and they learned to do it by doing it
badly in public and being corrected by strangers.

Every other kind of maintenance labour society depends on has a training
pathway, a ticket, a first-year apprentice wage. This one has a help page
nobody reads and a revert.

That is the gap this course exists to close, and the School of Invented
Disciplines would like it noted that we got here first.

## Outline

- the arithmetic: readers, articles, active editors, and the ratio between them
- four projects, one labour pattern: Wikipedia, Reddit, OSM, a package registry
- what "unpaid" does and does not mean — gift, hobby, obligation, second job
- the newcomer cliff: why roughly nine in ten first-time contributors never
  return after their first month
- what you will have done by week 12, and what you will have signed to do it
