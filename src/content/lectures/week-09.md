Nine volunteers, 900 removals a day, no training, no screening, no employee
assistance programme, and a queue that is worst at the hours when the fewest of
them are awake. This is a normal mid-sized moderation team and it describes
working conditions that would be illegal if anyone were being paid.

The failure modes are well documented and they arrive in an order. First the
team over-relies on the one member with insomnia. Then that member burns out
and leaves without a handover, taking the AutoModerator rationale with them.
Then the remaining team, short-handed, tightens the rules to reduce volume,
which increases appeals, which increases volume. Then somebody who enjoys the
power more than the work becomes the most active moderator by default, because
they are the only one still showing up.

That last step is how communities are captured, and it is not a conspiracy. It
is an attrition gradient.

The counter-measures are unglamorous and they are the actual content of this
week: caps on shift length, mandatory co-signature above a severity threshold,
rotation off the worst queues, a written record of why each rule exists, and
recruitment before you need it rather than after somebody quits.
