Two weeks into running a queue, the team argues about the rota. This is on
schedule.

## Before the Shift

Pull the week 8 numbers: decisions per person, time of day, appeal rate, and
who did the most.

## In the Shift

Look at the distribution first. In every cohort that has run this course, one
person has done between three and five times the median. Find them. They are
usually pleased about it, and week 9's lecture explains why that is the problem
rather than the solution.

Design the rota against the failure modes rather than against the volume: caps,
rotation off the worst queue, a second signature above a severity threshold, a
named person who covers a no-show, and a review date.

Then the costing. Take your sandbox's hours, apply a real hourly rate, and put
a number on a semester of it. Scale the number to a community of two million.
Argue about who should be paying it and what would change if they did — this is
the one argument in the course with no answer on the answer sheet.

## Afterwards

The rota commences. It has a review date. Somebody will have to chair that.
