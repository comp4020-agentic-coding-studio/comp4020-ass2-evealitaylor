Two hours, most of it admin, and the admin is the point: you are about to be
turned loose on communities of real people who did not consent to being
coursework.

## Before the Shift

Read the [policies](/policies/) page in full, particularly the contributor
undertaking. It is short and it is binding.

## In the Shift

Create your account. Use a name you are willing to keep — your contribution
history is public, permanent and attached to it, and a throwaway handle signals
exactly what you think of the people reading it.

Choose your home project from the four we support: English Wikipedia,
OpenStreetMap, a subreddit of your choosing with more than 10,000 subscribers,
or an open-source repository with a published contributing guide. You will stay
with it until week 12. Declare it and say in two sentences why — "it was the
easiest" is an acceptable answer if it is the true one.

Then find the public record: the modlog, the deletion log, the recent-changes
feed, the closed pull requests. Every remaining week of this course depends on
you being able to read what a community has already refused.

## Afterwards

You leave with a signed undertaking, a declared project, and nothing edited.
Editing is week 3. The two weeks in between are on purpose.
